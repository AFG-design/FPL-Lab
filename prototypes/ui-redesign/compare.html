Three directions for FPL LAB

Refined, Premium Analytics, and Modern & Interactive, each built as a working prototype on a real FPL LAB snapshot (GW11, team BACK2BACK) and screenshotted next to the live site. Nothing in the app, model or data pipeline was changed.

Sample data. Prototypes read a frozen copy of the published data.json from 18 Nov 09:02. Mini-league rivals are anonymised. Nothing is live.

The three concepts

A · REFINED

Same app, tidied

  • Same palette and fonts. Spacing on a 4px scale, with one type scale (12 to 32px).
  • Header is one row. The deadline is the only coloured number. The status line is gone.
  • Desktop home: a short verdict, then the pitch on the left and a decision rail on the right, so everything fits in the first screen.
  • Captain shown as a bar chart with a "close call · 0.5 xP" margin. Players filters fit on one row.
B · PREMIUM ANALYTICS

Built like an analyst's desk

  • Graphite surfaces, top tab bar and a KPI ribbon with a rank-by-GW sparkline.
  • Decision board: each call with its expected value and margin, in one table.
  • Squad and Players as xP heat grids by GW. Master-detail player panel with a 10th–90th percentile range.
  • Strategy line chart and a plan matrix comparing strategies GW by GW. Chart colours pass the colour-blind validator.
C · MODERN & INTERACTIVE

Touch-first and tactile

  • Floating icon rail on desktop, glass dock on phones, and a deadline ring.
  • Home pitch has a strategy switch and a GW scrubber. Tap GW12 to see the plan's team, with new signings badged.
  • Swipeable decision cards. Captain shown head to head. Players as cards with xP bars and a fixture strip.
  • Player drawer slides in on desktop and works as a bottom sheet on phones. Motion respects reduced-motion settings.

Recommendation: A, plus three pieces from B and C

Refined fixes the problems that matter most: the team above the fold, a one-row header, filters out of the way. It keeps the look the site already has, and it can be done in small CSS-led PRs against the existing site/styles tokens. B and C each have a few parts worth taking. Rebuilding the whole app in either of them isn't worth the cost.

  1. From B, the decision board. Each call, its xP and its margin in one block, replacing the three "Coin flip" cards. Also B's xP heat cells and its 10th–90th percentile range in the player sheet. The data for both already exists (xp by GW and dist).
  2. From C, the GW scrubber and strategy switch on the home pitch. It shows the plan as a team, not a list of moves, and it reuses the planner data already loaded.
  3. From C, the phone bottom sheet with a drag handle for the player view, plus press feedback and reduced-motion handling across the app.

Keep from today

  • The "Why 4.7 xP" breakdown (minutes, attack, bonus, cards) in the player sheet. None of the prototypes show it yet, and it has to stay.
  • Data-health banner, Refresh and Sync, and the AFG, Model and Data health tabs in their current places (CLAUDE.md).
  • Cards/Kits lineup toggle, saved views, presets and every filter. They move behind a Filters button but aren't removed.

Not prototyped

  • Deadlines, Chips, Review, You vs the model, Model, Data health, FPL School and AFG. These would be restyled with the shared tokens.
  • Live matchday states and the Rivals edge cases that npm run rivals covers.
  • C's fixtures view shows only the first match of a double gameweek, and badges without names on phones.
