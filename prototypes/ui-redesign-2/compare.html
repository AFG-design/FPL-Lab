The three directions

What each one changes, and what it costs

All three keep the identity that stayed through round 1: deep green surfaces, pitch-green actions, armband yellow only for the captain, the deadline and your own players, Barlow Condensed for titles and numbers, and the same sections in the same order.

A · Refined

Today's design, finished. The same tokens and layout, with the audit's open items fixed and one rule for headings.

Design choices

  • The page title is the only <h1>; the team name moves to the sidebar card.
  • Phone header: a 56px bar (title, deadline chip, search) plus a 52px stat strip that scrolls away. Content starts at 113px instead of 272px.
  • One type scale (11.5 / 13 / 14.5 / 17 / 28px) and one card-title style.
  • "Close call" pill only when the top two captains are within 1 xP, with the margin printed on it.
  • Planner view tabs 2×2 on phones; club crest plus 3-letter code in sticky columns; fades on any strip that scrolls sideways.

Strengths

  • Fixes both High audit items and most Mediums.
  • Lowest risk: same components, same muscle memory.
  • Mostly CSS and the header template.

Weaknesses

  • Looks like today's app. It is a polish pass, not a new look.
  • Desktop density barely changes.

B · Premium Analytics

An analyst's desk: thin rules instead of cards, tabular figures, and colour used only to encode data.

Design choices

  • KPI ribbon with sparklines: rank, points, plan xP, bench points, model correlation.
  • Home is a decision board (call, key number, alternatives) plus a squad xP matrix: 15 players × 5 GWs, heat cells and each player's likely range.
  • Players as a screener with a detail panel beside it at 1440px; no sheet to open and close.
  • Player view: outcome band, a stacked "Why 4.9 xP" bar, points vs xGI by gameweek.
  • Model: correlation by GW, a calibration plot, top-30 projected vs actual.

Strengths

  • The most information above the fold; best for comparing many players.
  • Every chart labelled and to scale.
  • Fits the tool's purpose: decisions from numbers.

Weaknesses

  • Heavier to read on a phone: tables scroll sideways and the header is 163px with the ribbon.
  • New layout on every screen, so the biggest build.
  • Feels like a terminal; less of the pitch-side warmth.

C · Modern & Interactive

The week as a guided flow, with choices that update the numbers as you make them.

Design choices

  • Home walks through 3 steps (Transfers, Captain, Lineup). Picking a captain re-marks the pitch and updates the XI total.
  • GW scrubber and strategy switch on the pitch; cards settle with a short stagger.
  • "/" or ⌘K command palette; a compare tray for up to 3 players.
  • Tabbed player sheet (Outlook, Form, Fixtures) with a waterfall showing how the xP adds up.
  • Rivals as a points race; tap a rival to see "they have, you don't".

Strengths

  • Shortest phone header (60px) and the largest tap targets.
  • Makes the "what if" of a choice visible straight away.
  • Motion only where it explains a change, all off under reduced motion.

Weaknesses

  • Lowest density: more scrolling to see the same numbers.
  • Tabs hide Form and Fixtures behind a tap.
  • Larger radii and the ring hero drift furthest from today's look.

Scorecard

Readability, density, phones, accessibility, effort

Dots are my judgement out of 5. The numbers under them were measured with the same script on every screen at 390px and 1440px, in both themes (prototypes/ui-redesign-2/measure.py).

CriterionTodayA · RefinedB · AnalyticsC · Interactive

Accessibility counts: text under 11px · text below WCAG AA against its solid background · controls under 24×24px (WCAG 2.5.8). The checker skips text on gradients or images rather than passing it (14 today, 21 A, 0 B, 71 C phone), so C's pitch and hero text still need a manual check. A's 20 desktop hits are 16px checkboxes inside a 24px label, which is the real target.

Recommendation

Build A, and take four pieces from B and C

A as the base. Worth it.

A fixes both High audit items (the phone header and the hidden Health column) and nearly all the Mediums without changing how the app works. It costs the least, and it brings the phone header down from 272px to 113px, the single biggest everyday gain. B and C are better at specific jobs, so take those jobs, not their whole look.

Worth combining

  1. From B: the Players screener with a detail panel at 1280px and wider. You can compare players without opening and closing a sheet. Phones keep the sheet.
  2. From B: the Model calibration plot and top-30 table. They show where the model misses (big hauls are under-projected) better than a single correlation line.
  3. From C: picking a captain updates the XI total and the pitch. The close-call captain decision then shows its cost in points, not just two numbers side by side.
  4. From C: the "How 4.9 xP adds up" waterfall in the player sheet, in place of today's list. Negative parts (cards) and the total read at a glance.

Not worth taking

  • B's KPI ribbon: it adds 50px back to the phone header, which is the problem A solves.
  • B's rules-only panels everywhere: great for tables, but harder to scan on Home.
  • C's 3-step stepper as Home's main layout: the decisions rail already does this, and today's site already has "/" search and a compare tool.
  • C's tabbed sheet and larger radii: more taps, and the furthest from today's look.
